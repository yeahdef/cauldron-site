Cauldron

The life counter that prints tokens.

[ iPhone and iPad ] [ Android ] [ Discord ] [ Instagram ]

Welcome to the Cauldron home page. Cauldron is a card game life counter for two to eight players. It sits in the middle of the table and keeps score. When somebody makes a 2/2 Zombie, you hold a button, say "two two zombie" out loud, and a Zombie comes out of a small thermal printer a few seconds later.

That is the whole idea, in a nutshell. Cauldron can print any token, a copy of any card, your own photographs and drawings, hidden roles for the table, a whole booster pack, or three packs each for a draft of eight. However, it does a whole lot more. This website lists it all.

What it prints is meant to be used and recycled. A printed token costs a fraction of a cent and takes a few seconds. Write on it, tear it in half when it dies, and drop the whole pile in the recycling at the end of the night. Or keep it, color on it. Make it fun. That is what makes it better than a die on a scrap of paper, and different from a box of tokens you can never find what you need when you need it.

It runs on iOS and Android. It is free. There are no ads, no accounts and no data collection. You do not need a printer to use it as a life counter. The token printing is the standout feature, but it also has some interesting life counter features not seen elsewhere.

The board

The board is the screen you will look at all night: one seat for each player, each a color of its own, each turned to face the person sitting at it. The phone lies flat in the middle of the table. Everything a player needs is on their own seat, the right way up for them.

Two players or a pod. Hold the phone upright for a two-player duel. Turn it on its side for a pod. Cauldron asks before it switches from one to the other, so handing the phone across the table never resets a game. And even if that does happen, we have a solution. see Time Travel

Pod size. Pods run from 3 to 8 players. Say six players, or set it in settings. The bigger pods need a wider screen, so a phone offers fewer sizes than a tablet does. At 3, 5 or 7 players there is one open seat left over on the board. It belongs to nobody, and it is not wasted: see When you are out of the game.

Starting life. Configure in settings, or say starting life thirty. Duels and pods each remember their own total, so 20 for a duel and 40 for Commander both stay put, until you want to change them.

Game clock. A small timer by the dial shows how long the game has been going. Say toggle clock to hide or show it.

New game. Swipe three fingers across the board. Everybody goes back to their starting life and every counter is cleared.

Winning. When one player is left standing, the board announces the winner, with a fanfare, and offers a new game.

It stays awake, and it remembers. A life counter is looked at all the time and touched hardly ever, so the screen does not dim or lock while the board is up. And the game itself is kept in memory. Close the app, the phone restarts, or somebody takes a call on it, the board comes back exactly as it was.

Life totals

Each seat is one big number, large enough to read from across the table. The whole seat is the button.

  • Tap the upper half of your seat to gain 1. Tap the lower half to lose 1. "Upper" is from where you are sitting, so it is the same for everyone.
  • Tap with two fingers to move by 5 at a time.
  • Hold one finger and the total keeps moving, faster the longer you hold. Good for the turn someone gains 37.

You can see what just happened. When a total changes, a faded copy of the old number and the size of the delta (such as −7) hang beside the new one for a moment. Nobody has to ask "wait, what were you at?"

Going out. When a player reaches zero their seat says DEAD. If it was poison or commander damage that caused it, the seat says that as well, so there is no argument later about how it happened.

Commander damage

Commander damage is the thing most life counters make you dig through a menu for. Here it is one simple movement: drag one finger from the attacker's seat into the defender's.

A tally appears on the defender's seat, on the edge facing whoever dealt it, so a glance at your own seat shows who has hit you and for how much. The life comes off at the same time, so you never have to enter the same hit twice. Each further drag adds one more. You can drag, hold, and tap with an additional finger to increment the damage in one motion. Tap a tally to take one back if it was a mistake.

Partners. If a player has two commanders, say "partners" and the color of their seat: partners blue. Their damage is then kept as two numbers, one for each commander. Start the drag from the *upper* half of their seat for one commander and the *lower* half for the other. Say it again to turn it off.

Counters and designations

There are no buttons for these. Forget digging into menus. We don't do that with Cauldron. You draw a letter on a seat with one finger, as if writing on the table, and that seat gets the counter. A drawn shape carries its own meaning in a way that digging through a menu never does, and it keeps the board free of clutter.

NOTE: The shapes are not all the obvious letters, and that is on purpose. Each was chosen because a finger can draw it quickly and the board can tell it apart from all the others.

Dice and coins

Dice are always within reach, and they roll in the middle of the board where the whole table can see the result.

  • Hold two fingers anywhere on the board: roll a d6.
  • Hold three fingers: roll a d20.
  • Say it for anything else: roll a d12, roll a d100, flip a coin. Sizes are d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20 and d100.
  • Planechase: roll the planar die.

The most recent 12 rolls stay in a ring around the button, like a clock, so "who rolled highest?" can be settled by looking, after everyone has rolled. The oldest result has a rectangle around it.

Time Travel

Somebody taps the wrong seat. Somebody swipes three fingers and wipes the game. It happens. Cauldron lets you wind the game backwards.

Hold the dial in the middle and circle it with your finger, like winding a clock back. The first full turn counterclockwise arms it and the dial reads UNDO. After that, every half turn steps the game back one move, and the board shows you the past as you go. Turn clockwise to come forward again. Lift your finger when the board looks right and that is where the game carries on from. We have time travel now, this is heavy.

A step is one whole change, not one tap: holding a finger down to lose 12 life is a single step, not twelve. And it reaches back through a new game into the game before, so an accidental reset is not the end of the world.

Printing tokens by voice

This is what Cauldron was made for. You do not look anything up or type anything during a game. You say what you need, the way you would say it to the person across the table.

  1. Press and hold the dial in the middle of the board (it reads SPEAK THY TOKEN).
  2. Wait for the rising beep.
  3. Say the token. Keep holding until your words show on screen.
  4. Let go. The card appears on the board, its name is read out in a robot voice from another decade, and it prints.

What to say

  • By what it is: two two zombie, two two black zombie knight with menace, one one dog. Power and toughness, color, creature types and abilities, in whatever order they come out of your mouth.
  • By name: treasure, food, clue, and every other named token.
  • Copies of a card: copy scute swarm prints a copy token of that card, with its art and its words.
  • Emblems and dungeons print too.
  • You do not need to say "token" at the end.

Real art, and a different one each time. Most tokens have been illustrated many times over the years. By default, Cauldron prints one of them at random, so a board full of Zombies is not a board of the same Zombie. You can choose which illustrations it draws from: see the token index.

The line underneath. Under each token is its color identity, written out in words (RED GREEN), because a thermal printer produces no pigment and it matters which Elemental is which.

More copies. Tap the card on screen for another copy, up to five more. Making six Goblins is one word and five taps.

It works offline. Cauldron keeps its own index of every token on the phone, so finding one does not need a connection. Once a printer is paired it also prepares and keeps the artwork ready to print, so a basement, a convention hall or an airplane is no obstacle. Settings shows how much is stored.

Tokens that were never printed. Some tokens have never had an official card of their own. The Cauldron Art Crew, a group of volunteer artists, draws them, and each one is numbered and credited to whoever drew it. They include Banana, Giant Bird, Zeppelin, Tetravite, Djinn, Goblin Scout, Camarid, Urami, Elemental Cat, etc. It grows until we design them all.

More things to say

The dial listens for more than tokens. All of these are said the same way: hold the dial, wait for the beep, speak, let go.

Your own photo for a token. Add "camera" after any token: one one dog camera. The camera opens, you take a photograph, and it prints at once as that token. Your own dog can be every Dog token you ever make.

A photo of anything. Say camera on its own to take a picture and print it just as it is.

A copy of a card, with a difference. Plenty of cards make a copy of a creature that is not quite the creature. Hashaton's copy is a 4/4 black Zombie. The Jolly Balloon Man's is a 1/1 with flying and haste. Uugguu makes two, each a 2/2. Say copy camera and the camera opens with a list down its right side of every card that copies this way, about 120 of them by name, each with what its copy changes. Then:

  1. Choose the card that is making the copy. The shutter waits until you have.
  2. Frame the card being copied. The frame on screen is the exact shape of a card.
  3. Tap the shutter. The photo prints with the change set under it, such as 4/4 BLACK ZOMBIE, so nobody has to remember what the copy is.

If the creature is on the other side of the table and upside down to you, tap 180° first and it prints the right way up. A card that makes more than one copy at a time prints that many from the one photo. The copy effects you use most stay at the top of the list in a group of their own, and a strip of letters down the edge jumps to any other card by name.

Draw a token. Say freestyle and the board becomes a sheet of paper. Draw anything with your finger and print it. Lots of potential here.

Creature labels. Small slips for the things that happen to a creature and are written nowhere on the card. Say the word and lay the slip on the creature: Goaded (or goaded by red, to say who did it), Phased Out, Suspected, Monstrous, Renowned, Adapted, Summoning Sick, Exerted, Stun, Indestructible, Shield, Finality, Prepared, Trample, Flying, Deathtouch, First Strike, Double Strike, Vigilance, Hexproof, Lifelink, Menace, Reach. Each label carries a one-line reminder of the rule, in case you've forgotten what Suspected does (I have no idea and I wrote the app).

Floating mana. Say floating mana for a WUBRG strip. Put a small die on each color you are holding, and stop trying to remember it through somebody else's turn.

Planechase. planeswalk turns over the next plane from a planar deck that Cauldron shuffles for you, sized for your table, and prints it. "Plane" plus a name prints one in particular: plane Academy at Tolaria West. With roll the planar die, that is a whole game of Planechase with no deck to carry. Omit/Include planes in the Token Index.

Momir Vig. momir five prints a random creature with mana value 5. The format where every creature is a surprise, finally played on paper.

Calling Mr. Vig. Momir is a whole format, so it has a whole screen. Say calling Mr. Vig and the board turns over into a payphone, the stainless kind from the nineties, with Momir himself behind the glass. Dial a mana value on the keypad and send the call, and a random creature of that value prints. Every turn after that is the same few taps, with nothing said out loud, which suits a noisy table. Hang up and the board is back the way you left it.

Favorites. print favorites prints every artwork you have hearted, one after another.

Share the app. QR download prints a slip with the store links on it, to hand to the person who has been asking about the printer all night.

Setup by voice. connect printer, settings, token index, card index, six players, starting life thirty.

The token index

Hold four fingers on the board, or say token index. The board turns over, and on the other side is the full catalog: every token there is, every illustration of each, and the place where you decide what Cauldron prints for you.

Find. Type in the search line and narrow it with the strip of filters below. Search by token name, by the card that makes it, or by artist. Each token shows exactly what to say to ask for it out loud, which is the quickest way to learn how to get what you want.

Choose your artwork. Swipe sideways through all the illustrations of a token. PRINT prints the one in the middle. And you can teach Cauldron your taste:

  • Tap an artwork to take it out of the random pool, for the one you never want to see again. Tap again to put it back.
  • Long-press an artwork to always print that one (★). Every Treasure you make will be your preferred Treasure.
  • RESET puts everything back as it was.

How the random pick works. A switch chooses SHUFFLE (every artwork once before any repeats), truly RANDOM (can repeat), or SEQUENTIAL. Another keeps Universes Beyond art out of random picks, for tables that would rather their Soldiers were not from a TV show.

Your own artwork. Swipe to the + card at the end of a token's gallery, then choose PHOTOS or CAMERA. A switch sets the token's name and rules in type under your picture, or prints the picture as it is.

Your own tokens. CREATE makes a token that does not exist anywhere else. Give it a name, a type and its oracle text, and add a picture afterward if you like. From then on it is in the index with all the others: it turns up in searches, it can be hearted, and you can ask for it by name with the dial. For the homebrew cube, the custom commander, or an inside joke.

Favorites. Drag the ♥ onto an artwork to heart it. From your favorites you can print one, print them all, or share them as a single picture or a short video.

The card index, and booster packs

Say card index. The token index is every token, this is every card: tens of thousands of them, each with every illustration it has ever had. Proxies at the kitchen table, trying a deck before buying it, and for that card you left at home you meant to put in your deck.

Find. Search by name. Filter by color and by set.

Three ways to print a card.

  • ART CROP: the illustration, with the card's name, cost, type and rules set in clear type under it. The easiest to read on thermal paper.
  • FULL CARD: the whole card as it was printed, frame and all.
  • TEXT ONLY: the words and nothing else. The least paper and the quickest.

Two-sided cards. A card with two sides prints as two pieces of paper, the front and then the back.

Print queue. Add cards to a queue as you browse, see the whole list, and print it in one go. A deck list can be brought in with IMPORT, so a hundred-card Commander deck does not have to be found a card at a time. EXPORT takes the list out again.

Booster packs. In the list of sets, a set that was sold in boosters has a PACK button. Press it and Cauldron deals one booster of that set, at that booster's real odds (the right number of commons, uncommons and a rare, with the right chance of something better) and prints it. Open a pack of a set that has been out of print for twenty-five years, for the price of a few feet of receipt paper.

Cards printed this way are for playing with friends and trying things out. They are thermal paper, they are smaller than a real card, and nobody will ever mistake one for the real thing. These are not for sale, they are playtest cards.

Game modes

Two ways to play a pod that need special cards, which is exactly what a printer in the middle of the table is good at. Both are set in settings, on one row. Turning one on turns the other off.

Treachery

The hidden-role variant from mtgtreachery.net, for pods of 4 to 8. One Leader, known to all. Guardians who protect the Leader, Assassins who want the Leader dead, and a Traitor who wants everybody dead, none of them known to anyone. Normally it needs a deck of identity cards and somebody to deal them. Cauldron is both.

Say treachery, or choose it in settings: simple is the four roles only, advanced uses all 62 identity cards with their own abilities.

  1. Cauldron deals every seat a role in secret.
  2. The dial reads DRAG FOR ROLE. Everyone else closes their eyes. Hold the dial, drag to your own seat, and let go. Your card prints, for you alone.
  3. Keep your card face down. Flip it over when you want to unveil.
  4. Once every seat has its card, the dial goes back to printing tokens.

The Leader's seat gets a green outline once their card has printed. When a player is out, the board shows what they were. And the board knows who is on which side, so it announces the side that wins.

Archenemy

One player against the whole table, for pods of 3 to 8. The archenemy has double the life and a deck of schemes, oversized cards that do something dreadful every turn. Cauldron keeps the scheme deck, so nobody has to own one.

Say archenemy, or choose it in settings.

  1. Everyone starts at 20 and the dial reads DRAG FOR ARCHENEMY.
  2. The archenemy holds the dial and drags to their seat. They go to 40 and their seat is outlined.
  3. On each of the archenemy's turns, say scheme to print the next card from their scheme deck.

The team wins when the archenemy is out. The archenemy wins when the whole team is.

If something goes wrong. An open cover, an empty roll or a printer that has gotten too warm does not lose your print. Cauldron tells you what the matter is, in words, and carries on once it is fixed.

You do not need the printer manufacturer's proprietary app. Cauldron talks to the printer directly. If the maker's app is running, close it before printing: it stays connected to the same printer and prints can come out blank. Cauldron warns you when it sees another app holding the printer.

Blank paper. If paper feeds but nothing is printed on it, flip the roll over. Only one side of thermal paper prints.

Gesture guide. Settings can print a small reference card with every gesture and phrase on it. Leave it on the table for the first few games.

How Cauldron stays free. Please use the links in the app when you buy a printer or paper. They are Amazon affiliate links, and they are the whole of how the app is paid for. That is how it can have no ads and never charge you for new features in a nickel-and-dimey way. Next time you need new sleeves, buy a pack from the app?

Settings

Swipe two fingers across the board, or say settings. There is no gear icon on the board, on purpose: the board is only the game. The first time you open Cauldron, a short tutorial teaches you this swipe before anything else.

  • Board format: auto-rotate between duel and pod, or hold it to one.
  • Pod size and starting life.
  • Treachery and Archenemy.
  • Printer: scan and pair, paper width, and how dark it prints.
  • Offline cache: how much is stored on the phone.
  • Speech language: US, UK, Australia, Canada, India and other English regions, so it hears your accent.
  • the printable gesture guide, and the way in to the token index and the card index.
  • Feedback and bug reports. Printer details are sent along with your report, so a problem can be found.

When you are out of the game

In a pod, somebody is always the first to die, and then they sit and watch for forty minutes. Cauldron has something for them.

Tap the DEAD plate on your seat. Cauldron asks whether the match was fun. If you say no, you get a small game to play on your own seat until the match ends. They are quiet, so the table can go on playing, and there are several of them. The open seat at a table of 3, 5 or 7 offers one too. There's a few secret games to try and find. Some are fun for everyone :)

How to run a successful Cauldy's Chaos Draft

A chaos draft is a draft where every pack is from a different set: anything, from any year. It is a huge amount of fun and almost nobody can afford to do it, because the old packs are now prohibitively expensive.

In Cauldy's Chaos Draft the packs are printed. A wheel holding every booster there has ever been is spun for each player, three times. Whatever it stops on is dealt at that booster's real odds and comes out of the printer in a wrapper with the player's number on it. A full 8 player draft pod costs about $1 in receipt paper

What you need

  • 4 or 8 players. The app runs a draft for either. With 8, everybody takes one card a pick as usual. With 4, everybody takes two cards per pick.
  • A NETUM NT-861 printer, paired in Cauldron. It is the one printer that cuts cards to sleeve size. Without it the wheel still spins, and nothing prints.
  • Paper. Each pack is about 3.5 feet of 80mm (3⅛ inch) roll. A 4-player draft uses about 42 feet and an 8-player draft about 84. One ordinary 230 foot roll does five 4-player drafts or two 8-player ones. Put in a fresh roll if you are not sure.
  • Card sleeves, standard size, 45 for each player, and three more each to hold their wrapped packs shut.
  • 45 real cards for each player that nobody minds using as stiffeners: commons or basic lands.
  • A charged phone, plugged in if you can. It has to stay awake with Cauldron open while a pack prints.

The method

Printed cards are thin paper, a little smaller than a real card. Sleeved in front of a real card, they shuffle, pass and play like the real thing. Do the sleeving before anybody sits down and the evening basically runs itself.

Part one: the chaos

All of the spinning and printing happens first, in one go, with everybody standing around the phone. Nobody opens anything yet.

  1. Pre-sleeve 45 commons for each player, with the backs facing out. Put each common or basic land into a sleeve turned around, so that its back shows through the clear front. Nobody should be able to tell what the real card is, and nobody will mistake it for one of their picks. Each player gets a stack of 45.
  2. Assign someone as the pack-prepper. The packs come out of the printer in like 30seconds, someone needs to be in front of it to grab, fold them into an accordian, and wrap the slipcover around them. it's an honorable duty. if no one is there to catch the packs, you're adding chaos to chaos.
  3. Open the chaos draft. In Cauldron, say card index. Then press and hold the words CARD INDEX at the top of the screen and keep holding, for about six seconds, until the screen comes apart. Choose 4 or 8 and press BEGIN CHAOS.
  4. Spin for every pack. Pass the phone around. Each player in turn presses SUBMIT TO CHAOS, watches the wheel, and waits for their pack to print before the next player spins. It goes around the table three times: everybody's pack 1, then everybody's pack 2, then everybody's pack 3. Each pack takes about a minute to print, fold, and prep.
  5. Make up each pack as it comes out. The dark slip comes out first, then the cards, joined at the cuts like a strip of stamps. Fold the strip up on itself at the cuts. Wrap the dark slip around the stack so nothing shows, and slide the whole thing into a spare sleeve to hold it shut. The slip says whose pack it is, which booster it was, and when it was dealt. No peeking.
  6. Carry on until the app says CHAOS COMPLETE. Every player now has three wrapped packs in front of them, and the phone's work is done.

Part two: the draft

Now everybody sits down, and it is a draft like any other, pretty much.

  1. Everybody opens their first pack. Tear the cards apart at the cuts, and slip each one into the first 15 of your pre-sleeved commons, in front of the real card, so the printed card shows through the clear front.
  2. Draft pack 1 like normal. Pick a card, pass the rest to your left, and carry on until the pack is gone. With four players, pick two each time.
  3. Open your second pack, sleeve it into your next 15 pre-sleeved commons, and draft it, passing to the right this time.
  4. And once more with your third pack and your last 15, passing to the left.
  5. Build decks and play. Forty cards is the usual size for a draft deck. Basic lands come from your own collection. I guess you can print those too, if you'd like.

Things worth knowing

  • Not every pack is 15 pieces of paper. Some old boosters were smaller, some new ones are bigger, and a card with two sides prints as two pieces. Sleeve a handful of spare commons per player so nobody runs short.
  • Two-sided cards. The line under each side says SIDE 1 OF 2 or SIDE 2 OF 2. They are one card. Put both in the same sleeve, side 1 in front, and pass them as one pick.
  • Everything is in English. A few real boosters held cards in Japanese, Phyrexian or Elvish. Cauldron deals the English printing of those, because on a card this size being able to read it matters more.
  • No pack comes up twice in the same draft.
  • Wait for PACK COMPLETE. The voice says it when a pack has finished printing. Do not lock the phone or switch apps while one is printing.
  • If the printer stops (no paper, an open cover), fix it and press CARRY ON PRINTING. The pack picks up where it stopped and nothing is dealt again.
  • It is loud on purpose. There is music, and it gets more excited each round. Turn the phone's volume down if you don't like it.
  • There are three very-unlikely-to-encounter joke packs. One is VERY GOOD. One is VERY NOT GOOD. One is, interesting?

Easter eggs
